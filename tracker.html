Understand your cycle

Menstrual phaseThe bleed · usually days 1–5

The uterine lining sheds, which is your period. Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest.

Cramps, tiredness and a low mood are common. For many people energy starts to come back towards the end. A period usually lasts 2–7 days.

Follicular phaseBuilding up · from day 1 until ovulation

Your brain signals the ovaries to grow follicles, each holding an egg, and estrogen rises. Many people notice more energy, a clearer head and a better mood.

With PCOS, follicles often stall instead of maturing, which is one reason this phase can run long.

OvulationEgg release · roughly mid-cycle

One follicle releases an egg, typically around the middle of a 28-day cycle. Some people notice clear, stretchy discharge or a mild one-sided twinge.

With PCOS, ovulation can be infrequent or skipped, so you can't assume a day. Pulse doesn't try to predict it.

Luteal phaseThe wind-down · usually 12–14 days

After ovulation, progesterone rises to prepare the lining. If there is no pregnancy, hormones drop and the next period begins.

In the days before a period, some people get bloating, breast tenderness, cravings, poorer sleep or mood dips. This is often called PMS.

Flow levelsFrom spotting to heavy

Spotting: a few drops, not enough to need a pad. Light: a pad or tampon a few times a day, only partly soaked.

Medium: a regular pad or tampon fills over several hours. Heavy: it fills quickly, or you wake at night to change it.

Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours is worth raising with a doctor promptly.

Cycle lengthHow it is counted

A cycle runs from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. 21–35 days is the typical range for adults.

Cycles outside that range, or very unpredictable ones, are worth discussing with your doctor. A log like this makes that conversation easier.
